Ally, Everyday

This is an independent concept exploring a few ways a digital bank could make everyday banking easier to understand and give customers more ways to share feedback.

Idea 1 — Banking, Explained: Create a recurring short-form education series that breaks down common banking questions—such as pending transfers, account features, and everyday money movement—in plain language.

Idea 2 — The Community Panel: Invite a rotating group of customers to share feedback on digital banking experiences through structured polls, listening sessions, and opt-in research. Summarize themes without exposing personal account information.

Idea 3 — You Said. We Heard.: Publish periodic updates showing which customer themes have been heard, what the organization is reviewing, and what actions—if any—have been taken. Clearly distinguish completed changes from ideas still under consideration.

How the ideas could work together: Start with a small 90-day pilot using social content and voluntary customer feedback. Review what people respond to before deciding whether to expand it.

What I would look at: Saves and shares, poll participation, feedback submissions, repeat participation, and whether people find the information easier to understand.

What this project explores: Business ideas, customer experience, audience feedback, social content, and ways to measure interest.

Independent portfolio concept only. Not commissioned by, affiliated with, or endorsed by Ally Financial. These are proposed ideas, not existing Ally programs, customer commitments, or announced product changes.

02 · Sports marketing · Academic observation

Charlotte Hornets Game Experience: Persuasion Techniques Used

Project overview: An observation-based analysis of persuasion techniques I noticed while attending a Charlotte Hornets game over Thanksgiving break, when the Hornets played the Miami Heat.

Ticket purchasing — scarcity and anticipation: Limited-availability ticket messaging created urgency and encouraged quick decisions. Social posts, email reminders, and countdowns also built anticipation before game day.

Arena atmosphere — social proof: Fans wearing team gear, cheering, and shopping for merchandise created a collective environment. Seeing other fans display and purchase Hornets gear made buying merchandise feel like part of belonging to the event.

Nostalgia and emotional connection: A montage of historic Hornets moments connected merchandise to team memories and identity, making a throwback jersey more appealing.

Merchandise — price anchoring and identity: Higher-priced memorabilia made lower-priced shirts and hoodies seem more affordable by comparison. Team apparel also served as an expression of loyalty and community.

Player endorsements: Videos featuring players promoting merchandise and food used athletes’ visibility and influence to make products more appealing.

Concessions — reciprocity and exclusivity: A complimentary Hornets towel illustrated reciprocity, while arena-specific items such as the Buzz City Burger made concessions feel distinctive to the event.

Contests and in-game advertising: A text-in trivia contest encouraged participation and kept fans engaged during a timeout. Arena-screen advertisements repeatedly exposed fans to food and merchandise promotions during breaks in play.

Halftime promotions and bundling: A limited-time 20% team-store discount created urgency. A Family Pack combining drinks, hot dogs, and popcorn presented a convenient offer and the perception of savings.

Sensory marketing: Food aromas, colorful merchandise displays, music, and sound effects made the arena more immersive and could increase fans’ interest in concessions and merchandise.

Post-game communication: A thank-you email and invitation to share photos extended the interaction beyond the game and helped maintain a connection with attendees.

Overall analysis: The game demonstrated how persuasion techniques operate across the entire fan journey—from ticket purchasing and arena entry to concessions, halftime promotions, and post-game communication. The techniques worked together to shape attention, participation, emotional connection, and potential spending. I enjoyed the experience, and it was difficult not to consider spending money because persuasive messages and opportunities appeared throughout the evening.

Skills demonstrated: Consumer behavior analysis, sports marketing observation, audience engagement, critical thinking, and written communication.

03 · Brand experience

Miami Heat Fan Experience Audit

Project goal: Review the fan experience at the Miami Heat’s April 19, 2024 home play-in game.

What stood out to me: In-game entertainment, the arena atmosphere, and digital tools such as mobile ticketing and social media were strengths. Ticket prices, parking costs, inconsistent promotions, late arrivals, and transportation access were areas that could be improved.

Ideas to consider: More personalized offers, better digital experiences, and sustainability initiatives. The audit also considered competition from other entertainment options, economic pressures, and changing fan expectations.

My recommendations: Consider flexible ticket options and value packages, make in-game promotions more consistent, improve arrival and parking information, and keep safety planning visible.

What I included: A SWOT analysis, an observation checklist, supporting visuals, and recommendations.

What this project explores: Customer experience, observation, SWOT analysis, research, and recommendations.

Academic observational audit based on the April 19, 2024 game. Findings and recommendations reflect the original assignment, not current verified operating conditions. Not commissioned by or affiliated with the Miami Heat.
